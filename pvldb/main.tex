\begin{abstract}
Many applications cannot tolerate a missed true neighbor. Examples include
deduplication, medical and legal retrieval, and scientific analysis. These
applications need \emph{exact} nearest-neighbor search. The standard tool
for fast exact search is a cheap lower bound on each candidate's distance:
if the bound already exceeds the best distance found so far, the candidate
can be discarded without computing its full distance. This paper builds
such bounds from truncated PCA projections. These bounds are tight when
variance concentrates in a few dimensions. We further observe that they
belong to a general family: any region that provably contains a set of
points yields an exact lower bound, and different region shapes are tight
for different data structures. We therefore compose two complementary
families into a single exact cascade. The first is PCA truncation, which
is tight for linearly correlated data. The second is a triangle-inequality
ball bound around $k$-means centroids, which is tight for clustered data.
The ball bound is exact for \emph{any} choice of centers, so clustering
quality affects only speed, never correctness.

Neither bound family helps on all data. The index therefore configures
itself from a small sample. The cascade widths are chosen from the
eigenvalue curve; fixed widths tuned for 128 dimensions misread
1536-dimensional OpenAI embeddings as unprunable, while widths placed at
fixed variance targets recover them. A three-tier diagnostic then decides
which levels to build: the eigenvalue curve and two cheap statistics
nominate, and a measured trial of the real cascade decides. We report
results in hardware-independent work units alongside wall-clock time,
because wall-clock proved regime-dependent while work counts never moved.
On four real datasets spanning 96 to 1536 dimensions (SIFT1M, GIST1M,
DBpedia-OpenAI-1M, and DEEP10M), exact 1-NN search performs
$3.9$--$20.8\times$ less work than a full scan. The wall-clock gain grows
with dimensionality, from $2.6\times$ at 128 dimensions to $6.2\times$ at
960. Exact $k$-NN generalizes the pruning radius to the $k$-th order
statistic; it wins up to $k\approx10$ and loses in RAM at $k=100$. Out of
core, the fetch set concentrates in few $k$-means clusters (27 of 1024 on
the embeddings). A measured two-tier layout with \texttt{O\_DIRECT} cold
reads answers exact queries $7$--$17\times$ faster than a full disk scan
and reads $40$--$730\times$ fewer bytes. We also compare against
ADSampling, the state-of-the-art statistical pruner. In RAM the work
counts are mixed. Out of core, however, ADSampling must touch every
vector because of its 32-dimension floor, while our cascade fetches only
0.06--1\% of the vectors. Exactness is verified on every query of every
experiment. All code, diagnostics, and experiments are public.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Finding the exact nearest neighbor in a large, high-dimensional dataset is
limited by the cost of computing full distances. Approximate methods such
as LSH~\cite{lsh}, HNSW~\cite{hnsw}, and IVF~\cite{faiss} trade recall for
speed. However, some applications cannot accept a missed true neighbor.
Examples include deduplication, medical and legal retrieval, and
scientific analysis. For these applications, the alternative is exact
pruning in the GEMINI tradition~\cite{gemini}: compute a cheap \emph{lower
bound} on each candidate's distance, and discard the candidate if the
bound already exceeds the best distance found so far. Our starting point
is such a bound from truncated PCA projections (\S\ref{sec:pca}). After
the data is rotated onto its principal axes, the distance between
$k$-dimensional prefixes is a lower bound on the true distance. A cascade
of progressively finer prefixes, seeded by a dynamic Top-$K$ probe, prunes
over 99.9\% of the SIFT1M dataset and returns provably exact
results.\footnote{Preliminary versions of this work appeared as two
self-archived reports (doi:10.5281/zenodo.21387358,
doi:10.5281/zenodo.22199955). This paper merges, extends, and supersedes
them. The $k$-NN generalization, the eigen-curve width selection, three of
the four datasets, the measured out-of-core results, and the ADSampling
baseline are new.}

That bound has a structural blind spot. PCA truncation is tight exactly
when variance concentrates in a few principal directions, which means
linearly correlated data. Clustered data can behave very differently. Its
eigenvalue spectrum can be flat, which collapses the PCA cascade toward
brute force. Yet the same data is highly compressible in another sense:
points huddle near a modest number of centroids. No single bound family
serves all data. Worse, a practitioner has no way to know, before building
an index, which family (if any) their data supports.

This paper makes two moves. First, \textbf{exact bounds compose}. The
maximum of valid lower bounds is itself a valid lower bound, so bound
families with complementary blind spots can share one cascade. We add a
triangle-inequality \emph{ball bound} around $k$-means centroids. It is
the clustered-data complement of PCA truncation, and it is exact for
\emph{any} choice of centers: clustering quality affects only pruning
power, never correctness. Second, \textbf{the composition is chosen by
measurement}. A three-tier diagnostic runs on a sample in seconds. It
rates the PCA levels from the eigenvalue decay, and it detects structure
beyond the covariance with two cheap statistics. We show that statistics
alone mispredict in both directions. The diagnostic therefore settles the
question with a measured trial of the real cascade, and emits an
\textsc{add}/\textsc{skip} verdict for each level.

There is also an economic motivation. An exhaustive flat scan requires the
entire dataset to be resident in RAM, which is the most expensive byte in
the machine. RAM costs roughly an order of magnitude more than NVMe per
byte. The pruning cascade needs only its metadata resident: 8--40 bytes
per vector, versus 512 bytes per vector for \texttt{fp32} data. At one
billion vectors, this is 8--40\,GB of RAM versus 512\,GB. That is a
commodity machine versus a specialty one, with exactness preserved.

Our contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Exact hierarchical pruning from composable bounds.} A
  PCA-prefix cascade with a dynamic Top-$K$ pruning radius, unified with
  the triangle-inequality ball bound under one containing-region
  principle. Each bound family covers the other's structural blind spot
  (\S\ref{sec:theory}).
\item \textbf{A composed exact cascade, generalized to $k$-NN.} The
  cascade combines a ball level and the PCA levels, with a radius
  re-probe. Exact $k$-NN uses the $k$-th order statistic over evaluated
  points as the pruning radius. Storage overhead is about 8 bytes per
  point (\S\ref{sec:algorithm}).
\item \textbf{Self-configuration that includes the cascade widths.}
  Widths are placed at fixed cumulative-variance targets on the
  eigenvalue curve; a fixed ladder is silently calibrated to one
  dimensionality and misreads others. The three-tier funnel then rules
  on each level, with verdicts derived from measured work ratios.
  Negative verdicts are included (\S\ref{sec:funnel}).
\item \textbf{Full-scale evidence on four real datasets} (96--1536
  dimensions, $10^6$--$10^7$ points), reported in hardware-independent
  work units. Wall-clock serves as cost-model validation. Negative
  controls and sample-to-full-scale checks are included
  (\S\ref{sec:eval}).
\item \textbf{A measured out-of-core design.} We measure the fetch set's
  cluster locality, build a two-tier on-disk layout, and run cold-read
  benchmarks under \texttt{O\_DIRECT}. Exact search is $7$--$17\times$
  faster than a full disk scan (\S\ref{sec:ooc}).
\item \textbf{An ADSampling baseline} in a shared work currency. It
  locates the honest boundary between statistical and deterministic
  pruning (\S\ref{sec:ads}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and Related Work}

\textbf{Lineage.} Neither bound family is new, and we do not claim
otherwise. Lower-bounding by dimensionality reduction descends from the
GEMINI framework~\cite{gemini} and the VA-file~\cite{vafile}. Pruning by
the triangle inequality around reference points is the basis of ball
trees~\cite{balltree}, vantage-point trees~\cite{vptree},
GNAT~\cite{gnat}, and Elkan's accelerated $k$-means~\cite{elkan}. The
approximate cousins are FAISS IVF~\cite{faiss}, which uses the same
$k$-means geometry without exactness, and OPQ~\cite{opq}, a per-cell
rotation that corresponds to the ellipsoid rung of the shape ladder in
\S\ref{sec:theory}. Disk-resident ANN systems such as
DiskANN~\cite{diskann} and SPANN~\cite{spann} target the same
memory-economics regime as we do, but they are approximate. The closest
method in mechanism is ADSampling~\cite{adsampling}. It also evaluates
incremental prefix distances after a rotation, but it replaces the
deterministic bound with a sequential hypothesis test under a
\emph{random} rotation. Its layout-level successor is PDX~\cite{pdx}. We
measure against ADSampling directly in \S\ref{sec:ads}. Our contribution
is threefold: the \emph{exact composition} of the two bound families in
one cascade, the \emph{measured self-configuration} including the cascade
widths themselves, and a diagnostic that also tells the practitioner when
to walk away.

\section{Composable Exact Bounds}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{The PCA Truncation Bound}
\label{sec:pca}

Let $X, Y \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and let $X', Y'$ be their images under the
orthogonal rotation onto the dataset's principal axes. Rotation preserves
the squared Euclidean distance,
$L_2^2(X, Y) = L_2^2(X', Y') = \sum_{i=1}^{d} (x'_i - y'_i)^2$.
Let $T_k$ retain the first $k$ coordinates.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:pca}
For any truncation width $k < d$,
$L_2^2\!\bigl(T_k(X'), T_k(Y')\bigr) \le L_2^2(X, Y)$.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The true squared distance is a sum of $d$ non-negative terms; the truncated
distance is the sum of the first $k$ of the same terms, and
$\sum_{i=k+1}^{d} (x'_i - y'_i)^2 \ge 0$.
\end{proof}

If the prefix distance to a candidate already exceeds the current search
radius $r$, the candidate is discarded without computing its full
distance. This is the GEMINI no-false-dismissal
condition~\cite{gemini}. Two choices turn the bound into an algorithm.
The first choice is the rotation. Any orthogonal rotation satisfies
Lemma~\ref{lem:pca}. PCA makes the bound \emph{tight}: by the
Karhunen--Lo\`eve theorem, it packs the maximum variance into the first
$k$ coordinates, and therefore, on average, the largest share of any
pairwise distance (quantified in Eq.~(\ref{eq:tightness})). In other
words, exactness is unconditional but quality is not. This separation
recurs throughout the paper. The second choice is the source of the
radius. Instead of a static threshold, a Top-$K$ probe at the coarsest
width computes exact distances for the $K = 50$ most promising candidates
and sets $r$ to their minimum. The prefix distance is a lower bound, so
this radius is safe from the first comparison on. No prior knowledge of
the neighbor distance is needed. A cascade of progressively finer
prefixes then re-prunes the survivors at each width. On SIFT1M, widths
$(8,16,32,64)$ discard all but an average of 564 of $10^6$ vectors before
any full-dimensional evaluation. Section~\ref{sec:funnel} replaces these
fixed widths with widths derived from the data's own eigenvalue curve.

\subsection{The Containing-Region Principle}

Let $R \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$ be any region that provably contains a set of
points $C$. Then for every $p \in C$ and any query $q$,
\begin{equation}
\lVert q - p \rVert \;\ge\; \mathrm{dist}(q, R),
\end{equation}
so $\mathrm{dist}(q,R)$ is an exact pruning bound for every member of $C$
at once. A bound family is therefore a \emph{shape} family, and a shape is
admissible when (a) containment is guaranteed and (b) $\mathrm{dist}(q,R)$
is cheap. Table~\ref{tab:shapes} lists the natural ladder. Each geometric
family is the twin of a quantizer family: a quantizer's
codeword-plus-distortion model \emph{is} a containing region. We use the
first two rungs; the others are available under the same principle.

\subsection{The Ball Bound}

Cluster the dataset with $k$-means. For each point $p$ assigned to centroid
$c$, store the scalar $d_p = \lVert p - c \rVert$ once, offline. At query
time, compute the $k$ centroid distances $\lVert q - c_j \rVert$ (with
$k \ll N$), and the triangle inequality gives, for every point,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ball}
\lVert q - p \rVert \;\ge\; \bigl|\, \lVert q - c_{a(p)} \rVert - d_p \,\bigr|,
\end{equation}
where $a(p)$ is $p$'s cluster. Evaluating (\ref{eq:ball}) for all $N$ points
requires one gather and one subtraction per point---\emph{no distance
computations}. A coarser, per-cluster form uses the cluster radius
$R_j = \max_{p \in j} d_p$: if $\lVert q - c_j \rVert - R_j$ exceeds the
current radius, the entire cluster is eliminated in one comparison, which in
an out-of-core layout skips a whole contiguous block (\S\ref{sec:layout}).

\subsection{Exactness Is Unconditional; Quality Is Not}

Inequality~(\ref{eq:ball}) is the triangle inequality, so it holds for
\emph{any} reference points. $k$-means enters only because it minimizes
the mean $d_p^2$, which makes the bound \emph{tight}. Three corollaries
follow. First, stale centers remain correct as the dataset drifts.
Pruning degrades gracefully, and re-clustering can wait until a
diagnostic calls for it. Second, insertion is $O(1)$: assign the new
point to its nearest existing centroid and store $d_p$. No re-clustering
is required for correctness. Third, the composed index mirrors the PCA
bound's property from \S\ref{sec:pca}. There, any orthogonal rotation is
exact and PCA makes it tight. Here, any centers are exact and $k$-means
makes them tight. The maximum of lower bounds is a lower bound, so the
two families compose freely in one cascade.

\subsection{Complementarity}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_bounds.pdf}
\caption{The two exact bound geometries. Left: the PCA truncation bound is
the distance between the projections onto the first $k$ principal
components---tight when variance concentrates along few directions. Right:
the ball bound $\lVert q-c \rVert - d_p$ from the triangle
inequality---tight when points huddle near their centroids. Each is loose
exactly where the other is tight.}
\label{fig:bounds}
\end{figure}

The PCA truncation bound is tight if and only if the eigenvalue spectrum
decays steeply. This is a \emph{global, linear} property
(Figure~\ref{fig:bounds}, left). The ball bound is tight if and only if
points sit close to their centroids relative to the spread of
$\lVert q - c \rVert$. This is a \emph{local} property. It holds for
multimodal data, and more generally for data of low intrinsic dimension,
where $d_p$ and the centroid distances spread widely instead of
concentrating. The two blind spots are disjoint by construction. Our
clearest demonstration is a synthetic dataset of 512 tight clusters whose
centers span all dimensions. Its spectrum is nearly flat: 95\% of the
variance needs 111 of 128 dimensions, and the PCA cascade manages only
$2.1\times$. Yet the ball bound prunes this dataset to 3\% before a
single projection is computed.

\textbf{Tier 1 --- eigenvalue decay, which also \emph{picks} the
widths.} Fit PCA on a sample. The eigenvalue curve is not merely a
heuristic signal: in the PCA basis the coordinates of the difference
between two independently drawn points are uncorrelated with variances
$2\lambda_i$, so for typical candidate pairs
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tightness}
\frac{\mathbb{E}\,L_2^2\bigl(T_k(X'), T_k(Y')\bigr)}
     {\mathbb{E}\,L_2^2(X, Y)}
\;=\;
\frac{\sum_{i \le k} \lambda_i}{\sum_{i} \lambda_i},
\end{equation}
which is the cumulative explained variance at $k$. The curve therefore
reads directly as the \emph{expected fractional tightness} of the
width-$k$ bound on the population the cascade prunes. In this sense, each
dataset's curve is its prunability signature (Figure~\ref{fig:eigen}).
Two blind spots follow from the same statement, and each one motivates a
later tier. First, Equation~(\ref{eq:tightness}) is an expectation over
pairs, but pruning is a tail event: a candidate is discarded only when
its prefix distance clears the radius. Scalar summaries of the curve can
therefore mislead. For example, GIST1M needs 303 of 960 dimensions for
95\% variance, which looks like a flat spectrum by the usual standard.
However, its steep \emph{head} (48\% by eight components) is what the
early levels actually use, and the measured verdict is \textsc{strong}.
Second, the spectrum captures only second-order structure. Clustered
structure that no rotation can expose is invisible to it. Tier 2 screens
for that structure, and tier 3 settles the question.

Preliminary
versions of this work used a fixed ladder $(8,16,32,64)$. We discovered
that this ladder is silently calibrated to $d \approx 128$. On
1536-dimensional OpenAI embeddings, it stops at 4\% of the dimensions and
prunes almost nothing: 68\% of candidates survive the 64-D stage. As a
result, the funnel misreads clearly prunable data as hopeless. The fix
makes tier 1 constructive. We place one level at the dimension reaching
each cumulative-variance target $\{30, 50, 70, 85\}\%$, rounded up to a
multiple of 8 and capped at $d/2$. We measured this rule against
hand-tuned ladders on 100k samples, using the work ratio. The rule ties
on SIFT ($8.2\times$ vs.\ $8.5\times$). It \emph{wins} on GIST1M
($15.2\times$ vs.\ $9.3\times$) and on DBpedia-OpenAI ($8.2\times$ vs.\
$7.6\times$). A self-configuring index cannot have a hard-coded ladder.
The eigen curve nominates the widths, and the trial below still decides.

\textbf{Verdicts are work ratios, not survivor fractions.} We caught a
second dimension-calibration bug in the same way. Rating prunability by
the final survivor fraction implicitly assumes one dimensionality.
GIST1M's 6.9\% finalists scored ``poor'' under a threshold tuned for
SIFT. However, at $d=960$ the same 6.9\% means $9.3\times$ fewer terms,
because a scan always pays $n \cdot d$. The verdict now derives from the
measured work ratio (sample terms vs.\ $n \cdot d$), which is
dimension-aware by construction.

\textbf{Tier 2 --- beyond-covariance statistics.} Tier 2 computes two
cheap statistics on the PCA-rotated sample. The first is the mean excess
kurtosis of the components, which measures marginal non-Gaussianity. The
second is $\mathrm{sqdep}$, the mean $|\mathrm{corr}(z_i^2, z_j^2)|$ over
component pairs. It measures joint dependence that no rotation can
remove, with a finite-sample noise floor of about $\sqrt{2/n}$. These
statistics detect that structure beyond the covariance \emph{exists}.
They do not tell whether it is \emph{harvestable}.

\textbf{Tier 3 --- the measured trial.} Tier 3 fits the real hybrid index
on the sample and runs the real cascade twice, with the ball level on and
off. It reports the measured ratio and the ball stage's own pruning rate.
The verdict is \textsc{add} or \textsc{skip}.

Tier 3 must be the decider. The evidence is that the statistics
mispredict in \emph{both} directions. On SIFT, tier 2 fires:
$\mathrm{sqdep} = 0.032$ is $7\times$ the noise floor, so real
beyond-covariance structure exists. Yet the trial says \textsc{skip}
($1.0\times$), because the ball bounds prune only 50--59\% and the bound
work cancels the savings. On a latent-dimension-8 Gaussian, tier 2 is
clean: kurtosis is near 0 and $\mathrm{sqdep}$ sits at the floor. Yet the
trial says \textsc{add} ($1.9\times$), because low intrinsic dimension
spreads $d_p$ widely and the bounds prune to 23\% with zero
multimodality. Across all datasets, the measured value of the ball level
is a monotone function of its own pruning rate (Figure~\ref{fig:law}):
survivors of 3\% give $4$--$14\times$; 15--23\% give $2$--$3\times$;
50--59\% give parity; and 100\% gives $0.8\times$, a measured loss. The
trial measures exactly this quantity. No statistic substitutes for it.

\section{The Hybrid Algorithm}
\label{sec:algorithm}

\subsection{Offline}

The offline stage has three steps. First, fit PCA; the covariance is
accumulated in chunks, so no full \texttt{float64} copy of the dataset is
ever materialized. Second, select the cascade widths from the eigen curve
(\S\ref{sec:funnel}). Third, fit $k$-means with
$k = \mathrm{clip}(\sqrt{N}, 64, 4096)$, using MiniBatch (24\,s on SIFT1M
for $k{=}1024$). For each point we store a cluster id (\texttt{int32})
and $d_p$ (\texttt{float32}), 8 bytes in total, plus the $k \times d$
centroids and the per-cluster radii. The choice of $k$ is measured, not
tuned. A sweep over $k \in \{8, 64, 256, 1024, 4096\}$ on SIFT1M shows
ball-stage survivors falling monotonically from 81\% to 43\%, while fit
cost rises from 4\,s to 47\,s. $\sqrt{N}$ sits at the knee of this
gain-versus-cost curve, so it is the default.

\subsection{Query Pipeline}

\begin{enumerate}
\item $k$ centroid distances ($k \cdot d$ operations).
\item Ball bounds (\ref{eq:ball}) for all $N$ points: one gather and one
  absolute difference each.
\item \textbf{Probe}: exact distances to the \textsc{probe}$=50$ points with
  the smallest ball bounds seed a safe initial radius $r$ (the Top-50
  probe of \S\ref{sec:pca}, seeded by ball bounds instead of coarse
  projections).
\item Ball pruning: retain points with $\mathrm{bound}^2 < r^2$.
\item PCA cascade $(8,16,32,64)$ on the survivors, with a \textbf{re-probe}
  at the first PCA stage: before pruning, exact distances to the 50 best
  8-dimensional candidates tighten $r$. Without this step the ball-seeded
  radius is loose and the cascade degrades (8-D survivors 27.2\% versus
  14.6\% with it); with it, the cascade matches PCA-only tightness
  stage-for-stage.
\item Full-distance check on the final survivors. The result is provably
  identical to brute force, and is additionally verified against the
  official ground truth in every experiment.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Exact $k$-NN}
\label{sec:knn}

For $k$-NN, the pruning radius generalizes from the best evaluated
distance to the $k$-th smallest exact distance among the points evaluated
so far. The $k$-th order statistic over a \emph{subset} can only
overestimate the true one. Therefore, any point whose lower bound reaches
this radius provably cannot enter the $k$-NN set. One subtlety carries an
exactness bug if it is missed. The evaluated multiset can contain a point
twice, because the probe and re-probe sets overlap. The $k$-th order
statistic of a multiset with duplicates is biased low, and a radius that
is too small \emph{over}-prunes. The minimum (the 1-NN special case) is
not affected by duplicates, but the $k$-th order statistic is. The radius
must therefore be taken over \emph{distinct} evaluated points. The probe
size also grows to $\max(50, k)$, so that the initial radius is a valid
$k$-th statistic. Correctness is verified against brute force across
datasets, both cascade paths, and $k$ up to $n-1$. A second subtlety
surfaced during verification. Integer-valued descriptors make exact
distance \emph{ties} real: SIFT query~13 has two points at squared
distance exactly 61334, and only one of them is in the official ground
truth. $k$-NN sets are therefore unique only up to ties at the boundary.

\subsection{Out-of-Core Layout}
\label{sec:layout}

Pruning fragments I/O only if the layout ignores it. This is the classic
index-versus-scan crossover: hundreds of scattered small reads can lose
to one full sequential scan. Our two-tier design keeps \emph{resident}
only what pruning decisions touch. The resident tier holds the rotated
sketch of the first $\max(\mathrm{levels})$ PCA dimensions, the ball
metadata (\texttt{assign}${+}d_p$, 8 bytes per point), the centroids, and
the per-cluster offsets. Together this is $2$--$8\times$ smaller than the
data. Full-dimensional vectors live on disk in two interchangeable files.
The first keeps the original order, so each fetch is one small aligned
read. The second is \emph{cluster-major}: each $k$-means cluster is a
contiguous, page-aligned block (under 0.4\% padding), written
sequentially into \texttt{fallocate}-preallocated extents. The entire
cascade (probe, re-probe, and all pruning stages) runs on the resident
tier. The disk is touched only by the fetch set: probe, re-probe, and
finalists. Section~\ref{sec:ooc} measures exactly which points that is.

Two structural facts make the layout matter. First, the cluster-level
bound alone is a weak block-skipper: on SIFT1M, 40--52\% of clusters
survive it. Second, the fetch set is far more concentrated than uniform.
The nearest neighbors of a query are near each other, so they share
clusters, and the final fetch becomes a few large reads. This is the
wisdom of the VA-file~\cite{vafile}, SPANN~\cite{spann}, and
DiskANN~\cite{diskann}: scan small sketches sequentially, and fetch
survivors in a layout-aware way. The same $k$-means that supplies the
bound also supplies the layout. Section~\ref{sec:ooc} shows that it earns
its keep as a \emph{partitioner} even on datasets where the funnel
rejects it as a \emph{pruning level}.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\textbf{Metrics policy.} Primary claims are stated in
hardware-independent work units. The reason is that wall-clock proved
regime-dependent: the same algorithm measured $4.7\times$, $3.6\times$,
$2.7\times$, and parity under changes of numeric type, machine load, and
implementation tuning, while its work counts never moved (details below).
The primary metrics are: (1) full-vector fetches per query, which is the
out-of-core I/O currency; (2) total bytes touched per query,
\emph{including the cost of the pruning machinery itself}; (3) resident
RAM bytes per vector; and (4) the survivor cascade, which is the
algorithmic fingerprint. Wall-clock appears as validation of a
first-order cost model
($t \approx \mathrm{bytes}/\mathrm{bandwidth} +
\mathrm{ops}/\mathrm{throughput}$), with FAISS supplying the
tuned-constants reference. A reader can therefore plug their own
machine's constants into our work numbers and predict their own outcome.

\textbf{Setup.} Intel i9-9900K (8C/16T, AVX2${+}$FMA, no AVX-512),
dual-channel DDR4, 30\,GB usable RAM; NVMe SSD measured under
\texttt{O\_DIRECT} at 2.04\,GiB/s sequential (8\,MiB reads) and 80\,\textmu s
per random 4\,KiB read; \texttt{faiss-cpu} 1.15 dispatching its AVX2 build;
NumPy and OpenBLAS at the AVX2 tier---baseline and FAISS use the same
instruction set. SIFT1M in-RAM timings (Table~\ref{tab:sift}) are medians
of 5 repetitions on a quiet machine with warm cache; the multi-dataset,
$k$-NN, and out-of-core tables are medians or means over 100 queries in a
single repetition on a quiet machine (work counts and byte counts are
exact and run-invariant; only the millisecond columns carry run noise).
Exactness is verified on every query of every experiment---against
official ground truth where one exists, against in-harness brute force
otherwise. Results are CPU-governor-insensitive (medians within 2\%).

\textbf{Why wall-clock is the wrong headline (measured).} Two confounds

\subsection{Extrapolation}
\label{sec:extrap}

Does the sample predict the full dataset? On SIFT, the funnel's trial on
a 100k sample returns $1.0\times$ (\textsc{skip}), and the full-1M parity
benchmark measures exactly that (61.1 versus 62.2\,ms). The check extends
to every dataset. GIST1M sampled at $9.3\times$ work with the fixed
ladder and at $15.2\times$ with eigen-curve levels; the full-1M runs
measured $13.1\times$ and $20.8\times$ respectively, so the sample is
conservative. DEEP10M sampled \textsc{moderate} at $3.6\times$ and
measured $3.9\times$ at $10^7$ points. The ball level's sample
\textsc{skip} was confirmed at full scale on all four datasets. On GIST,
for example, 92\% of points survive the ball stage, the term counts are
identical, and the wall-clock is strictly worse. In short, the diagnostic
predicts full-scale outcomes from a sample. This includes the negative
verdicts, which is its most valuable behavior: it prevents building a
useless level.

There are three findings. \textbf{First, the headline.} On the OpenAI
embeddings, exact cold-read 1-NN takes 318\,ms against a 5.4-second scan,
a $17\times$ speedup. It reads 8\,MB instead of 5.9\,GB, which is
$730\times$ fewer bytes. The byte ratios are device-independent. On
storage that is slower or more seek-bound than NVMe, they translate into
even larger time ratios. \textbf{Second, the fetch set is highly
cluster-local.} The embeddings' $\sim$1{,}014 fetched points occupy a
median of 27 of 1024 clusters, while uniform placement would touch about
644. SIFT's 583 points occupy 52 clusters. The reason is that the nearest
neighbors of a query are near each other. This locality is what makes the
cluster-major arm viable at all. It also means the $k$-means structure
earns its place as a \emph{partitioner} even where the funnel rejects its
\emph{bound} (\textsc{skip} on all four datasets). The same geometry is
useless for compute but valuable for layout. \textbf{Third,
point-versus-block is a device property.} With serial reads at
80\,\textmu s, the point arm's few thousand small fetches beat the block
arm's fewer but heavier reads on three of the four datasets. Only
DEEP10M's 50k-point fetch set flips the order. Parallel small reads would
extend the point arm's advantage, since NVMe queue depths reward exactly
that pattern. Seek-bound media would flip every row toward block. The
layout writes both files, so the diagnostic should pick per device.

One design lesson surfaced only here: \emph{the optimal cascade depth is
deeper out of core than in RAM}. SIFT's eigen-curve levels $(8,16,40)$
minimize in-RAM terms, but they fetch 3{,}381 points. The deeper
$(8,16,32,64)$ ladder spends more sketch terms to fetch only 588 points,
and it wins the cold-read benchmark (61 vs.\ 91\,ms). In RAM, terms are
the currency. Out of core, fetched bytes are. The level-selection
objective should follow the medium.

ADSampling~\cite{adsampling} is the natural adversary. It also prunes
with incremental prefix distances after a rotation. However, its rotation
is random, so partial distances become unbiased samples, and its stopping
rule is a sequential hypothesis test with a tunable error rate
$\epsilon_0$. This buys harder pruning per dimension, at the price of the
exactness guarantee. For a fair comparison, we ported the reference
implementation into the same harness and the same currency: dimensions
touched equal terms. Table~\ref{tab:ads} is honestly mixed in RAM. We win
at 96--128 dimensions, ADS wins on the 1536-D embeddings, and GIST is a
tie. At $k{=}10$, ADS widens its advantage. On recall, we must correct
our own first measurement. An earlier float32 ground-truth check
miscounted near-duplicate points as recall failures, due to catastrophic
cancellation. Under float64 verification, ADS at the default $\epsilon_0$
achieved perfect recall on every $k{=}1$ query we ran. It missed one true
neighbor in 600 query-tasks (GIST, $k{=}10$, recall 0.9990). The
distinction that matters is therefore \emph{guarantee versus empirical},
not observed failure. The distinction is structural, because no
$\epsilon_0$ turns the test into a bound.

The decisive difference is not in RAM. ADS pays its $\Delta d = 32$ floor
for \emph{every} point in the dataset. SIFT's 33.0M terms and DEEP10M's
325.8M terms sit exactly at $32n$. ADS must therefore touch every vector,
and out of core it reads full-scan bytes regardless of its parameters.
Its successor PDX~\cite{pdx} restructures the layout dimension-major, but
it still visits every vector. Our cascade fetches only 0.06--1\% of the
vectors (Table~\ref{tab:ooc}). Finally, the two methods compose rather
than compete: a statistical test could accelerate the final full-distance
checks over our survivor sets, at the usual price.

\section{Limitations}

High \emph{intrinsic} dimensionality defeats every cheap bound. Distance
concentration flattens the eigenvalue spectrum and the spread of $d_p$ at
the same time. The funnel exists precisely to detect this case and say so
(Table~\ref{tab:regimes}, last row). DEEP10M sits near this boundary in
RAM. At native-code constants, the in-RAM wall-clock race belongs to
fused exhaustive scans (FAISS) at the 128-D scale. ADSampling's
statistical pruning also beats our deterministic bounds in RAM on the
highest-dimensional embeddings (\S\ref{sec:ads}). Exact $k$-NN loses the
in-RAM wall-clock by $k = 100$ on every dataset we measured
(\S\ref{sec:knn-eval}). The out-of-core measurements use
\texttt{O\_DIRECT} on datasets that fit in RAM. This is valid
device-price accounting, but a larger-than-RAM run, with parallel rather
than serial fetch I/O, remains future work. Updates are also future work:
the cluster-major layout assumes a static index that is rebuilt
periodically, which is the same assumption disk-ANN builds make. Finally,
ball-bound and layout quality depend on the $k$-means sample, as any
learned structure does. Correctness never does.

\section{Conclusion}

Exact lower bounds are shapes, and shapes compose. The right composition
is a property of the data, and it can be measured on a sample before any
index is built. On that principle, this paper delivers an exact $k$-NN
index that configures itself end to end. The cascade widths come from the
eigenvalue curve; without this step, 1536-dimensional embeddings
masquerade as unprunable. The levels come from a measured trial, and its
sample verdicts are confirmed at $10^6$--$10^7$ scale on four real
datasets. The breaking points are stated honestly: $k = 100$ in RAM, and
i.i.d.\ data everywhere. The out-of-core argument has become a
measurement: exact cold-read queries run $7$--$17\times$ faster than a
disk scan and read $40$--$730\times$ fewer bytes, against a
state-of-the-art statistical pruner that structurally cannot leave RAM.
The recurring lesson of this work is simple. Every constant we had
silently tuned to one dataset---the cascade widths, the verdict
thresholds, even our own ground-truth checker---failed on the next
dataset. Every fix was the same: derive the constant from a measurement.
Measure the data to choose the index; count the work to predict the time.
