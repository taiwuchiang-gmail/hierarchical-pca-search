\title{Exact Nearest-Neighbor Search with Composable Lower Bounds:
Self-Configuring Hierarchical Pruning, In RAM and Out of Core}

\author{Tai-Wu Chiang}
\orcid{0009-0004-7062-9346}
\email{twchiang@utexas.edu}
\affiliation{%
  \institution{Independent Researcher}
  \city{California}

\begin{abstract}
Many applications cannot tolerate a missed true neighbor. Examples include
deduplication, medical and legal retrieval, and scientific analysis. These
applications need \emph{exact} nearest-neighbor search. The standard tool
for fast exact search is a cheap lower bound on each candidate's distance:
if the bound already exceeds the best distance found so far, the candidate
can be discarded without computing its full distance. This paper builds
such bounds from truncated PCA projections. These bounds are tight when
variance concentrates in a few dimensions. We further observe that they
belong to a general family: any region that provably contains a set of
points yields an exact lower bound, and different region shapes are tight
for different data structures. We therefore compose two complementary
families into a single exact cascade. The first is PCA truncation, which
is tight for linearly correlated data. The second is a triangle-inequality
ball bound around $k$-means centroids, which is tight for clustered data.
The ball bound is exact for \emph{any} choice of centers, so clustering
quality affects only speed, never correctness.

Neither bound family helps on all data. The index therefore configures
itself from a small sample. The cascade widths are chosen from the
eigenvalue curve; fixed widths tuned for 128 dimensions misread
1536-dimensional OpenAI embeddings as unprunable, while widths placed at
fixed variance targets recover them. A three-tier diagnostic then decides
which levels to build: the eigenvalue curve and two cheap statistics
nominate, and a measured trial of the real cascade decides. We report
results in hardware-independent work units alongside wall-clock time,
because wall-clock proved regime-dependent while work counts never moved.
On four real datasets spanning 96 to 1536 dimensions (SIFT1M, GIST1M,
DBpedia-OpenAI-1M, and DEEP10M), exact 1-NN search performs
$5.4$--$26.9\times$ less work than a full scan. Against a NumPy
brute-force scan, the in-RAM wall-clock gain is $1.3\times$ at 96
dimensions, $2.7\times$ at 128, and $7.9$--$8.2\times$ at 960 and 1536;
a single-thread FAISS scan still wins in RAM at 128 dimensions. Exact
$k$-NN generalizes the pruning radius to the $k$-th order statistic over
a probe that grows with $k$; at $k=100$ the cascade still beats brute
force on all four datasets ($1.2$--$5.9\times$). Out of core, the fetch set concentrates in few $k$-means
clusters (27 of 1024 on average on the embeddings). A measured two-tier
layout with \texttt{O\_DIRECT} cold reads answers exact queries
$2.5$--$19\times$ faster than our full disk scan, or $1.4$--$11\times$
faster than the device's sequential-bandwidth limit for any scan, and reads
$18$--$730\times$ fewer bytes. We also compare against ADSampling, the
state-of-the-art statistical pruner. In RAM our cascade does less work
on three of the four datasets, and ADSampling wins on the 1536-dimensional
embeddings. ADSampling pays a 32-dimension floor on every vector, while our cascade
fetches only 0.05--1.2\% of the vectors; an equal-residency out-of-core
comparison remains future work. Exactness is verified on every query of
every in-RAM experiment. All code, diagnostics, and experiments are
public.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Finding the exact nearest neighbor in a large, high-dimensional dataset is
limited by the cost of computing full distances. Approximate methods such
as LSH~\cite{lsh}, HNSW~\cite{hnsw}, and IVF~\cite{faiss} trade recall for
speed. However, some applications cannot accept a missed true neighbor.
Examples include deduplication, medical and legal retrieval, and
scientific analysis. For these applications, the alternative is exact
pruning in the GEMINI tradition~\cite{gemini}: compute a cheap \emph{lower
bound} on each candidate's distance, and discard the candidate if the
bound already exceeds the best distance found so far. Our starting point
is such a bound from truncated PCA projections (\S\ref{sec:pca}). After
the data is rotated onto its principal axes, the distance between
$k$-dimensional prefixes is a lower bound on the true distance. A cascade
of progressively finer prefixes, seeded by a dynamic Top-$K$ probe, prunes
over 99.9\% of the SIFT1M dataset and returns provably exact
results.\footnote{Preliminary versions of this work appeared as two
self-archived reports (doi:10.5281/zenodo.21387358,
doi:10.5281/zenodo.22199955). This paper merges, extends, and supersedes
them. The $k$-NN generalization, the eigen-curve width selection, three of
the four datasets, the measured out-of-core results, and the ADSampling
baseline are new.}

That bound sees only linear structure. PCA truncation is tight on average
when variance concentrates in a few principal directions, which means
linearly correlated data. Clustered data can behave very differently. Its
eigenvalue spectrum can be flat, so the eigen curve predicts a loose
bound. Yet the same data is highly compressible in another sense: points
huddle near a modest number of centroids, and a bound built on those
centroids costs one subtraction per point rather than a multiply-add per
coordinate. No single bound family serves all data. Worse, a practitioner has no way to know, before building
an index, which family (if any) their data supports.

This paper makes two moves. First, \textbf{exact bounds compose}. The
maximum of valid lower bounds is itself a valid lower bound, so bound
families with different blind spots can share one cascade. We add a
triangle-inequality \emph{ball bound} around $k$-means centroids. It is
the clustered-data complement of PCA truncation, and it is exact for
\emph{any} choice of centers: clustering quality affects only pruning
power, never correctness. Second, \textbf{the composition is chosen by
measurement}. A three-tier diagnostic runs on a sample in seconds. It
rates the PCA levels from the eigenvalue decay, and it detects structure
beyond the covariance with two cheap statistics. We show that statistics
alone mispredict in both directions. The diagnostic therefore settles the
question with a measured trial of the real cascade, and emits an
\textsc{add}/\textsc{skip} verdict for each level.

There is also an economic motivation. An exhaustive flat scan requires the
entire dataset to be resident in RAM, which is the most expensive byte in
the machine. RAM costs roughly an order of magnitude more than NVMe per
byte. The pruning cascade needs only its sketch resident: the first
$\max(\mathrm{levels})$ rotated coordinates plus 16 bytes of per-point
metadata. On our four datasets this is $1.8$--$7.7\times$ less RAM than
the data (for example, 272 versus 512 bytes per vector on SIFT1M, and 496
versus 3{,}840 on GIST1M), with exactness preserved. Quantizing the sketch
with conservative cell bounds, as the VA-file does~\cite{vafile}, would
shrink it further; we leave that to future work.

Our contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Exact hierarchical pruning from composable bounds.} A
  PCA-prefix cascade with a dynamic Top-$K$ pruning radius, unified with
  the triangle-inequality ball bound under one containing-region
  principle. The two families exploit different structure: linear
  correlation and clustering (\S\ref{sec:theory}).
\item \textbf{A composed exact cascade, generalized to $k$-NN.} The
  cascade combines a ball level and the PCA levels, with a radius
  re-probe. Exact $k$-NN uses the $k$-th order statistic over evaluated
  points as the pruning radius. In RAM, the storage overhead is about 8
  bytes per point (\S\ref{sec:algorithm}).
\item \textbf{Self-configuration that includes the cascade widths.}
  Widths are placed at fixed cumulative-variance targets on the
  eigenvalue curve; a fixed ladder is silently calibrated to one
  dimensionality and misreads others. The three-tier funnel then rules
  on each level, with verdicts derived from measured work ratios.
  Negative verdicts are included (\S\ref{sec:funnel}).
\item \textbf{Full-scale evidence on four real datasets} (96--1536
  dimensions, $10^6$--$10^7$ points), reported in hardware-independent
  work units. Wall-clock serves as cost-model validation. Negative
  controls and sample-to-full-scale checks are included
  (\S\ref{sec:eval}).
\item \textbf{A measured out-of-core design.} We measure the fetch set's
  cluster locality, build a two-tier on-disk layout, and run cold-read
  benchmarks under \texttt{O\_DIRECT}. Exact search is $2.5$--$19\times$
  faster than our full disk scan, and $1.4$--$11\times$ faster than the
  device's sequential-bandwidth limit for any scan (\S\ref{sec:ooc}).
\item \textbf{An ADSampling baseline} in a shared work currency. It
  locates the in-RAM boundary between statistical and deterministic
  pruning (\S\ref{sec:ads}).
\end{enumerate}

\section{Background and Related Work}

\textbf{Lineage.} Neither bound family is new, and we do not claim
otherwise. Lower-bounding by dimensionality reduction descends from the
GEMINI framework~\cite{gemini} and the VA-file~\cite{vafile}. Pruning by
the triangle inequality around reference points is the basis of ball
trees~\cite{balltree}, vantage-point trees~\cite{vptree},
GNAT~\cite{gnat}, and Elkan's accelerated $k$-means~\cite{elkan}. The
approximate cousins are FAISS IVF~\cite{faiss}, which uses the same
$k$-means geometry without exactness, and OPQ~\cite{opq}, which learns a
global rotation. Its locally optimized variant LOPQ~\cite{lopq} rotates
each cell separately, which corresponds to the ellipsoid rung of the shape
ladder in \S\ref{sec:theory}. Disk-resident ANN systems such as
DiskANN~\cite{diskann} and SPANN~\cite{spann} target the same
memory-economics regime as we do, but they are approximate. The closest
method in mechanism is ADSampling~\cite{adsampling}. It also evaluates
incremental prefix distances after a rotation, but it replaces the
deterministic bound with a sequential hypothesis test under a
\emph{random} rotation. PDX~\cite{pdx} accelerates this style of pruning
with a dimension-major layout. We
measure against ADSampling directly in \S\ref{sec:ads}. Our contribution
is threefold: the \emph{exact composition} of the two bound families in
one cascade, the \emph{measured self-configuration} including the cascade
widths themselves, and a diagnostic that also tells the practitioner when
to walk away.

\section{Composable Exact Bounds}
\label{sec:theory}

\subsection{The PCA Truncation Bound}
\label{sec:pca}

Let $X, Y \in \mathbb{R}^d$ and let $X', Y'$ be their images under the
orthogonal rotation onto the dataset's principal axes. Rotation preserves
the squared Euclidean distance,
$L_2^2(X, Y) = L_2^2(X', Y') = \sum_{i=1}^{d} (x'_i - y'_i)^2$.
Let $T_k$ retain the first $k$ coordinates.

\begin{lemma}
\label{lem:pca}
For any truncation width $k < d$,
\[ L_2^2\bigl(T_k(X'), T_k(Y')\bigr) \le L_2^2(X, Y). \]
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The true squared distance is a sum of $d$ non-negative terms; the truncated
distance is the sum of the first $k$ of the same terms, and
$\sum_{i=k+1}^{d} (x'_i - y'_i)^2 \ge 0$.
\end{proof}

If the prefix distance to a candidate already reaches the current search
radius $r$, the candidate is discarded without computing its full
distance. This is the GEMINI no-false-dismissal
condition~\cite{gemini}. Two choices turn the bound into an algorithm.
The first choice is the rotation. Any orthogonal rotation satisfies
Lemma~\ref{lem:pca}. PCA makes the bound \emph{tight}: by the
Karhunen--Lo\`eve theorem, it packs the maximum variance into the first
$k$ coordinates, and therefore the largest expected share of the squared
distance between two random points (quantified in
Eq.~(\ref{eq:tightness})). In other
words, exactness is unconditional but quality is not. This separation
recurs throughout the paper. The second choice is the source of the
radius. Instead of a static threshold, a Top-$K$ probe at the coarsest
width computes exact distances for the $K = 50$ most promising candidates
and sets $r$ to their minimum. Because $r$ is the exact distance to an
actual data point, it is at least the true nearest-neighbor distance; and
because the prefix distance is a lower bound, pruning against such an $r$
never discards the true neighbor. The radius is therefore safe from the
first comparison on, and no prior knowledge of the neighbor distance is
needed. A cascade of progressively finer prefixes then re-prunes the
survivors at each width. Each level adds only its new coordinates to a
running prefix sum, so a candidate that survives to width $k$ has paid
$k$ terms in total, and the work counts in \S\ref{sec:eval} charge
exactly that. On SIFT1M, widths
$(8,16,32,64)$ discard all but an average of 564 of $10^6$ vectors before
any full-dimensional evaluation. Section~\ref{sec:funnel} replaces these
fixed widths with widths derived from the data's own eigenvalue curve.

\subsection{The Containing-Region Principle}

Let $R \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$ be any region that provably contains a set of
points $C$. Then for every $p \in C$ and any query $q$,
\begin{equation}
\lVert q - p \rVert \;\ge\; \mathrm{dist}(q, R),
\end{equation}
so $\mathrm{dist}(q,R)$ is an exact pruning bound for every member of $C$
at once. A bound family is therefore a \emph{shape} family, and a shape is
admissible when (a) containment is guaranteed and (b) $\mathrm{dist}(q,R)$
is cheap. Table~\ref{tab:shapes} lists the natural ladder. Each geometric
family is the twin of a quantizer family: a quantizer's
codeword-plus-distortion model \emph{is} a containing region. We use the
first two rungs; the others are available under the same principle.

\subsection{The Ball Bound}

Cluster the dataset with $k$-means. For each point $p$ assigned to centroid
$c$, store the scalar $d_p = \lVert p - c \rVert$ once, offline. At query
time, compute the $k$ centroid distances $\lVert q - c_j \rVert$ (with
$k \ll N$), and the triangle inequality gives, for every point,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:ball}
\lVert q - p \rVert \;\ge\; \bigl|\, \lVert q - c_{a(p)} \rVert - d_p \,\bigr|,
\end{equation}
where $a(p)$ is $p$'s cluster. Evaluating (\ref{eq:ball}) for all $N$ points
requires one gather and one subtraction per point---\emph{no distance
computations}. A coarser, per-cluster form uses the cluster radius
$R_j = \max_{p \in j} d_p$: if $\lVert q - c_j \rVert - R_j$ exceeds the
current radius, the entire cluster is eliminated in one comparison, which in
an out-of-core layout skips a whole contiguous block (\S\ref{sec:layout}).

\subsection{Exactness Is Unconditional; Quality Is Not}

Inequality~(\ref{eq:ball}) is the triangle inequality, so it holds for
\emph{any} reference points. $k$-means enters only because it minimizes
the mean $d_p^2$, which makes the bound \emph{tight}. Three corollaries
follow. First, stale centers remain correct as the dataset drifts.
Pruning degrades gracefully, and re-clustering can wait until a
diagnostic calls for it. Second, insertion costs $O(kd)$, independent of
$N$: assign the new point to its nearest existing centroid, store $d_p$,
and raise that cluster's radius if needed. No re-clustering is required
for correctness. Third, the composed index mirrors the PCA
bound's property from \S\ref{sec:pca}. There, any orthogonal rotation is
exact and PCA makes it tight. Here, any centers are exact and $k$-means
makes them tight. The maximum of lower bounds is a lower bound, so the
two families compose freely in one cascade.

A note on terminology. Throughout this paper, each pruning step of the
cascade is called a \emph{level}. The \emph{ball level} applies the ball
bound (\ref{eq:ball}) to all points. Each \emph{PCA level} applies the
prefix bound of Lemma~\ref{lem:pca} at one width. A query passes through
the levels in order, so we speak of a level's \emph{survivors}: the
candidates that its bound could not discard.

\subsection{Complementarity}

\begin{figure}[t]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{fig_bounds.pdf}
\caption{The two exact bound geometries. Left: the PCA truncation bound is
the distance between the projections onto the first $k$ principal
components---tight when variance concentrates along few directions. Right:
the ball bound $\lVert q-c \rVert - d_p$ from the triangle
inequality---tight when points huddle near their centroids. The two
bounds exploit different structure.}
\label{fig:bounds}
\end{figure}

The PCA truncation bound is tight when the eigenvalue spectrum decays
steeply. This is a \emph{global, linear} property
(Figure~\ref{fig:bounds}, left). The ball bound is tight when points sit
close to their centroids relative to the spread of
$\lVert q - c \rVert$. This is a \emph{local} property. It holds for
multimodal data, and more generally for data of low intrinsic dimension,
where $d_p$ and the centroid distances spread widely instead of
concentrating. The two conditions are different, not exclusive: data can
satisfy both or neither (i.i.d.\ noise, \S\ref{sec:regimes}). A synthetic
dataset of 512 tight clusters whose centers span all dimensions shows
both sides at once. Its spectrum is nearly flat (at $d=128$, 95\% of the
variance needs 110 of 128 dimensions), yet the PCA bound is not loose
there: the 8-D level alone leaves 0.2\% of the points, because pruning is
a tail event (\S\ref{sec:funnel}). What limits the PCA cascade to
$2.1\times$ ($n=200$k, $d=64$) is the cost of that first level, eight
multiply-adds for every point. The ball bound prunes the same data to
0.2\% with one subtraction per point, before a single projection is
computed.

\label{sec:funnel}

index have for \emph{my} data?'' Figure~\ref{fig:funnel} shows its shape:
two statistical tiers that may only \emph{nominate}, and one measured tier
that alone \emph{decides}.

\textbf{Tier 1 --- eigenvalue decay, which also \emph{picks} the
widths.} Fit PCA on a sample. The eigenvalue curve is not merely a
heuristic signal: in the PCA basis the coordinates of the difference
between two independently drawn points are uncorrelated with variances
$2\lambda_i$, so for typical candidate pairs
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:tightness}
\frac{\mathbb{E}\,L_2^2\bigl(T_k(X'), T_k(Y')\bigr)}
     {\mathbb{E}\,L_2^2(X, Y)}
\;=\;
\frac{\sum_{i \le k} \lambda_i}{\sum_{i} \lambda_i},
\end{equation}
which is the cumulative explained variance at $k$. The curve therefore
reads directly as the ratio of the expected squared width-$k$ prefix
distance to the expected squared full distance, over the population the
cascade prunes. In this sense, each
dataset's curve is its prunability signature (Figure~\ref{fig:eigen}).
Two blind spots follow from the same statement, and each one motivates a
later tier. First, Equation~(\ref{eq:tightness}) is an expectation over
pairs, but pruning is a tail event: a candidate is discarded only when
its prefix distance clears the radius. Scalar summaries of the curve can
therefore mislead. For example, GIST1M needs 303 of 960 dimensions for
95\% variance, which looks like a flat spectrum by the usual standard.
However, its steep \emph{head} (48\% by eight components) is what the
early levels actually use, and the measured verdict is \textsc{strong}.
Second, the spectrum captures only second-order structure. Clustered
structure that no rotation can expose is invisible to it. Tier 2 screens
for that structure, and tier 3 settles the question.

Preliminary
versions of this work used a fixed ladder $(8,16,32,64)$. We discovered
that this ladder is silently calibrated to $d \approx 128$. On
1536-dimensional OpenAI embeddings, it stops at 4\% of the dimensions and
prunes almost nothing: 68\% of candidates survive the 64-D level. As a
result, the funnel misreads clearly prunable data as hopeless. The fix
makes tier 1 constructive. We place one level at the dimension reaching
each cumulative-variance target $\{30, 50, 70, 85\}\%$, rounded up to a
multiple of 8 and capped at $d/2$. We measured this rule against
hand-picked ladders on 100k samples, using the work ratio: the fixed
$(8,16,32,64)$ ladder on SIFT and GIST1M, and a deeper $(32,64,128,256)$
ladder on DBpedia-OpenAI, where the fixed ladder measures only
$1.4\times$. The rule \emph{wins} on GIST1M ($19.6\times$ vs.\
$10.8\times$), ties the hand-deepened ladder on DBpedia-OpenAI
($12.6\times$ each), and loses slightly on SIFT ($10.1\times$ vs.\
$11.0\times$), where the 85\% target stops at 40 dimensions. No single
hand-picked ladder serves all three: the one that is best on SIFT
measures $1.4\times$ on DBpedia. A self-configuring index cannot have a
hard-coded ladder. The eigen curve nominates the widths, and the trial
below still decides.

\textbf{Verdicts are work ratios, not survivor fractions.} We caught a
second dimension-calibration bug in the same way. Rating prunability by
the final survivor fraction implicitly assumes one dimensionality.
GIST1M's 6.9\% finalists scored ``poor'' under a threshold tuned for
SIFT. However, at $d=960$ the same 6.9\% means $10.8\times$ fewer terms,
because a scan always pays $n \cdot d$. The verdict now derives from the
measured work ratio (sample terms vs.\ $n \cdot d$), which is
dimension-aware by construction.

\textbf{Tier 2 --- beyond-covariance statistics.} Tier 2 computes two
cheap statistics on the PCA-rotated sample. The first is the mean excess
kurtosis of the components, which measures marginal non-Gaussianity. The
second is $\mathrm{sqdep}$, the mean $|\mathrm{corr}(z_i^2, z_j^2)|$ over
component pairs. It measures joint dependence that the PCA rotation does
not remove. We compare it against $\sqrt{2/n}$, a conservative noise
threshold: for independent components its expected value is about
$\sqrt{2/(\pi n)}$, roughly 0.56 times as large. These statistics detect that structure beyond the covariance \emph{exists}.
They do not tell whether it is \emph{harvestable}.

\textbf{Tier 3 --- the measured trial.} Tier 3 fits the real hybrid index
on the sample and runs the real cascade twice, with the ball level on and
off. It reports the measured ratio and the ball level's own pruning rate.
The verdict is \textsc{add} or \textsc{skip}.

Tier 3 must be the decider. The evidence is that the statistics
mispredict in \emph{both} directions. On SIFT, tier 2 fires:
$\mathrm{sqdep} = 0.032$ is $7\times$ the noise threshold, so real
beyond-covariance structure exists. Yet the trial says \textsc{skip}
($0.9\times$), because 50--59\% of the points survive the ball bounds and
the bound work outweighs the savings. On a latent-dimension-8 Gaussian,
tier 2 is clean: kurtosis is near 0 and $\mathrm{sqdep}$ sits at its
independence value, below the threshold. Yet the trial says \textsc{add}
($1.6\times$), because low intrinsic dimension spreads $d_p$ widely and
the bounds prune to 25\% with zero multimodality. We call the ball level's measured value its
\emph{gain}: the cascade's wall-clock time without the ball level divided
by its time with it, so a gain above 1 means the ball level helps. Across
the eight datasets and trials of Figure~\ref{fig:law}, this gain falls
as the ball level's own survivor rate rises, monotonically except for
timing noise of a few percent near parity. Survivors of
0.2--4\% give gains of $4.7$--$5.8\times$; 13--25\% give
$1.6$--$2.6\times$; 49--59\% give $0.9$--$1.0\times$, parity or a slight
loss; and survivors near 100\% give $0.8$--$0.9\times$, a measured
loss. This is an empirical
trend at $d \le 128$, not a law: the level's cost per point is fixed,
while the savings it buys grow with $d$. The trial measures exactly this
quantity. No statistic substitutes for it.

\section{The Hybrid Algorithm}
\label{sec:algorithm}

\subsection{Offline}

The offline build has three steps. First, fit PCA; the covariance is
accumulated in chunks, so no full \texttt{float64} copy of the dataset is
ever materialized. Second, select the cascade widths from the eigen curve
(\S\ref{sec:funnel}). Third, fit $k$-means with MiniBatch (24\,s on
SIFT1M for $k{=}1024$). The default is
$k = \mathrm{clip}(\sqrt{N}, 64, 4096)$; the 100k-sample trials use it
($k=316$), while every full-scale experiment in \S\ref{sec:eval} fixes
$k = 1024$, including DEEP10M. For each point we store a cluster id
(\texttt{int32}) and $d_p$ (\texttt{float32}), 8 bytes in total, plus the
$k \times d$ centroids and the per-cluster radii. The default comes from
a measurement on one dataset. A sweep over
$k \in \{8, 64, 256, 1024, 4096\}$ on SIFT1M shows ball-level survivors
falling monotonically from 81\% to 43\%, while fit cost rises from 4\,s to
47\,s. $\sqrt{N}$ sits at the knee of this gain-versus-cost curve.

\subsection{Query Pipeline}

\begin{enumerate}
\item $k$ centroid distances ($k \cdot d$ operations).
\item Ball bounds (\ref{eq:ball}) for all $N$ points: one gather and one
  absolute difference each.
\item \textbf{Probe}: exact distances to the \textsc{probe}$=50$ points with
  the smallest ball bounds seed a safe initial radius $r$ (the Top-50
  probe of \S\ref{sec:pca}, seeded by ball bounds instead of coarse
  projections).
\item Ball pruning: retain points with $\mathrm{bound}^2 < r^2$.
\item PCA cascade on the survivors at the selected widths ($(8,16,32,64)$
  on SIFT1M), with a \textbf{re-probe}
  at the first PCA level: before pruning, exact distances to the 50 best
  8-dimensional candidates tighten $r$. Without this step the ball-seeded
  radius is loose and the cascade degrades (8-D survivors 27.2\% versus
  14.6\% with it); with it, the cascade matches PCA-only tightness
  level-for-level.
\item Full-distance check on the final survivors. In exact arithmetic the
  result is identical to brute force. It is additionally verified against
  brute force, and against the official ground truth where one exists.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Floating point.} The exactness argument holds in exact
arithmetic. In \texttt{float32}, rounding can make a computed bound
exceed the computed distance it bounds. The ball bound is the most
exposed: when $q$ nearly duplicates a point $p$ far from its centroid,
$\lVert q - c \rVert$ and $d_p$ nearly cancel, and their rounding errors
can dwarf $\lVert q - p \rVert$. This is not hypothetical. On synthetic
near-duplicate data (groups of copies that differ by one unit in the last
place in one coordinate, in four broad clusters), the unguarded ball test
returned a wrong 40-NN set on 20 of 150 queries. Every pruning test
therefore keeps a candidate unless its bound beats the radius by a
margin. For squared prefix sums the margin is a relative $10^{-5}$,
about five times the worst-case rounding error of a \texttt{float32} sum
of up to 2048 terms under NumPy's pairwise summation. For the ball bound
it is also absolute, $10^{-5}(\max_j \lVert q - c_j\rVert + \max_p d_p)$,
which covers the cancellation. With the margin, every in-RAM query,
near-duplicates included, returns exactly the distances of a
\texttt{float32} brute-force scan. On SIFT and GIST samples the margin
admits at most three extra candidates per query, under 0.01\% of the
survivors at any level.

\subsection{Exact $k$-NN}
\label{sec:knn}

For $k$-NN, the pruning radius generalizes from the best evaluated
distance to the $k$-th smallest exact distance among the points evaluated
so far. The $k$-th order statistic over a \emph{subset} can only
overestimate the true one. Therefore, any point whose lower bound reaches
this radius provably cannot enter the $k$-NN set. One subtlety carries an
exactness bug if it is missed. The evaluated multiset can contain a point
twice, because the probe and re-probe sets overlap. The $k$-th order
statistic of a multiset with duplicates is biased low, and a radius that
is too small \emph{over}-prunes. The minimum (the 1-NN special case) is
not affected by duplicates, but the $k$-th order statistic is. The radius
must therefore be taken over \emph{distinct} evaluated points. The probe
also scales with $k$: it evaluates 50 points per requested neighbor
($50k$), the 1-NN probe size per neighbor. Any probe of at least $k$
points gives a valid $k$-th statistic, but a probe of exactly $k$ makes
the radius the probe's maximum, the loosest valid choice. On SIFT1M at
$k=100$ (50 queries), the PCA-only cascade does 63\,M terms with a
100-point probe, 18.7\,M with 400 points, and 13.7\,M with 5{,}000; at
10{,}000 (14.0\,M) the probe costs more than it saves. Correctness is
verified against brute force across
datasets, both cascade paths, and $k$ up to $n-1$. A second subtlety
surfaced during verification. Integer-valued descriptors make exact
distance \emph{ties} real: SIFT query~13 has two points at squared
distance exactly 61334, and only one of them is in the official ground
truth. $k$-NN sets are therefore unique only up to ties at the boundary.

\subsection{Out-of-Core Layout}
\label{sec:layout}

Pruning fragments I/O only if the layout ignores it. This is the classic
index-versus-scan crossover: hundreds of scattered small reads can lose
to one full sequential scan. Our two-tier design keeps \emph{resident}
only what pruning decisions touch. The resident tier holds the rotated
sketch of the first $\max(\mathrm{levels})$ PCA dimensions, the ball
metadata (\texttt{assign}${+}d_p$, 8 bytes per point), an 8-byte row
index into the cluster-major file, the centroids, and the per-cluster
offsets. On our four datasets this is $1.8$--$7.7\times$ smaller than the
data (Table~\ref{tab:ooc}). Full-dimensional vectors live on disk in two interchangeable files.
The first keeps the original order, so each fetch is one small aligned
read. The second is \emph{cluster-major}: each $k$-means cluster is a
contiguous, page-aligned block (under 0.4\% padding), written
sequentially into \texttt{fallocate}-preallocated extents. The entire
cascade (probe, re-probe, and all pruning levels) runs on the resident
tier. The disk is touched only by the fetch set: probe, re-probe, and
finalists. Section~\ref{sec:ooc} measures exactly which points that is.

Two structural facts make the layout matter. First, the cluster-level
bound alone is a weak block-skipper: on SIFT1M, 40--52\% of clusters
survive it. Second, the fetch set is far more concentrated than uniform.
The nearest neighbors of a query are near each other, so they share
clusters, and the final fetch becomes a few large reads. This is the
wisdom of the VA-file~\cite{vafile}, SPANN~\cite{spann}, and
DiskANN~\cite{diskann}: scan small sketches sequentially, and fetch
survivors in a layout-aware way. The same $k$-means that supplies the
bound also supplies the layout. Section~\ref{sec:ooc} measures when this
pays: the cluster-major file beats per-point fetches only on DEEP10M,
where the fetch set is largest.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\textbf{Metrics policy.} Primary claims are stated in
hardware-independent work units. The reason is that wall-clock proved
regime-dependent: the same algorithm measured $4.7\times$, $3.6\times$,
$2.7\times$, and parity under changes of numeric type, machine load, and
implementation tuning, while its work counts never moved (details below).
The primary metrics are: (1) distance terms per query, with the pruning
machinery's own work charged separately as bound-ops; (2) full-vector
fetches and bytes read from storage per query, which are the out-of-core
I/O currency; (3) resident RAM bytes per vector; and (4) the survivor
cascade, which is the algorithmic fingerprint. Wall-clock appears as a
check on a first-order cost model
($t \approx \mathrm{reads} \cdot \mathrm{latency} +
\mathrm{bytes}/\mathrm{bandwidth} + \mathrm{ops}/\mathrm{throughput}$),
with FAISS supplying the tuned-constants reference. A reader can therefore plug their own
machine's constants into our work numbers and predict their own outcome.

\textbf{Setup.} Intel i9-9900K (8C/16T, AVX2${+}$FMA, no AVX-512),
dual-channel DDR4, 30\,GB usable RAM; NVMe SSD measured under
\texttt{O\_DIRECT} at 1.85\,GiB/s sequential (8\,MiB reads) and
83--117\,\textmu s per random 4\,KiB read (on the 0.5 and 3.6\,GiB
files); \texttt{faiss-cpu} 1.15 dispatching its AVX2 build;
NumPy and OpenBLAS at the AVX2 tier---baseline and FAISS use the same
instruction set. SIFT1M in-RAM timings (Table~\ref{tab:sift}) are medians
of 5 repetitions on a quiet machine with warm cache. The multi-dataset
and $k$-NN tables report mean ms/query over 100 queries in a single
repetition on a quiet machine. The out-of-core table reports median
ms/query and mean bytes and reads (Table~\ref{tab:ooc}). Work counts and
byte counts are exact and run-invariant; only the millisecond columns
carry run noise. In RAM, exactness is verified on every query of every
experiment---against in-harness brute force, and against official ground
truth where one exists. Out of core, the point and block arms are checked
against the scan arm on its 10 queries. Results are
CPU-governor-insensitive (medians within 2\%).

\textbf{Why wall-clock is the wrong headline (measured).} Two confounds
were caught only by asking why ratios changed between runs. The first is
numeric type. sklearn's PCA silently outputs \texttt{float64}, and the
bandwidth-bound brute baseline is $1.86\times$ more expensive in
\texttt{float64}. A $2\times2$ decomposition on identical code measures
the same cascade at $3.6\times$ in f64 and $2.7\times$ in f32. The f64
number matches the first preliminary report's regime and its reported
$4.3\times$. The second confound is implementation parity. An unnecessary
full-array gather in our own PCA-only path inflated the ball level's
apparent contribution to $2.0\times$. Fixing it revealed the tie in
Table~\ref{tab:sift}. Machine load adds a further multiplier: background
bandwidth contention slows the scan-bound baseline the most, which
flatters all pruners. Survivor counts were invariant through every one of
these changes. This is why we adopted the metrics policy above.

Figure~\ref{fig:work} makes the contrast with SIFT concrete in the work
currency. On SIFT the hybrid does somewhat less counted work, 4.9\% of
a scan in terms plus 0.8\% in bound-ops against 8.1\% for PCA-only, and
the wall-clock still ties, because each bound-op is a gather rather than
a streaming term. On the clustered data the hybrid does far less work:
12.8\% against 0.5\% in terms plus 1.6\% in bound-ops, so
most of its small cost is the ball bound-ops, not distance terms. This is what a survivor-count plot cannot
show: both cascades reach the same survivors, but the hybrid reaches them
with much less work.
On the latent-48 blob, the ball level is measured to \emph{hurt}
($0.8\times$): 100\% of points survive, so the level is pure overhead. On
i.i.d.\ noise, the diagnostic correctly reports that nothing works and
that brute force or ANN should be used. The i.i.d.\ row also shows the
PCA cascade itself losing to brute force ($0.5\times$). It even does more
terms than a scan ($0.7\times$ work reduction), because 88\% of the
points reach the final check, which recomputes all $d$ coordinates. That is
the honest boundary of the entire approach.

\subsection{Extrapolation}
\label{sec:extrap}

Does the sample predict the full dataset? On SIFT, the funnel's trial on
a 100k sample returns $0.9\times$ (\textsc{skip}), and the full-1M parity
benchmark measures a tie (59.4 versus 59.9\,ms). The check extends
to every dataset. GIST1M sampled at $10.8\times$ work with the fixed
ladder and at $19.6\times$ with eigen-curve levels; the full-1M runs
measured $15.3\times$ and $26.9\times$ respectively, so the sample is
conservative. DEEP10M sampled \textsc{moderate} at $5.0\times$ and
measured $5.4\times$ at $10^7$ points. The ball level's sample
\textsc{skip} was confirmed at full scale on all four datasets. On GIST,
for example, 92\% of points survive the ball level, the term counts are
nearly identical (36.1 vs.\ 35.7\,M), and the wall-clock is strictly
worse. In short, the diagnostic
predicts full-scale outcomes from a sample. This includes the negative
verdicts, which is its most valuable behavior: it prevents building a
useless level.

Section~\ref{sec:layout} motivated the two-tier layout from structure.
Table~\ref{tab:ooc} now measures it end to end. The protocol is simple:
all reads use \texttt{O\_DIRECT} with page-aligned buffers. The page
cache is bypassed, so every query pays device prices. This is a
cold-cache protocol with no cache-flushing ceremony. Our scan baseline,
however, does not overlap I/O with compute: it reaches 1.0--1.1\,GiB/s,
against the device's 1.85\,GiB/s. A scan pipelined to the device limit
would take 258\,ms on SIFT1M, 1.93\,s on GIST1M and DEEP10M, and 3.1\,s on
DBpedia-OpenAI. Against that limit, the faster arm's speedups are
$3.3\times$, $2.6\times$, $11.3\times$, and $1.4\times$ (block, on
DEEP10M), respectively.

There are three findings. \textbf{First, the headline.} On the OpenAI
embeddings, exact cold-read 1-NN takes 274\,ms against a 5.1-second scan,
a $19\times$ speedup ($11.3\times$ against the device's sequential
limit).
It reads 8\,MiB instead of 5.7\,GiB, which is $730\times$ fewer bytes. The
byte ratios are device-independent. How they translate into time depends
on how much a device could stream in the time of one random read: about
165\,KB on this NVMe drive (83\,\textmu s at 1.85\,GiB/s), but about
1.5\,MB on a disk with a 10\,ms seek and 150\,MB/s of bandwidth. Seek-bound media therefore favor
the scan and the block arm, not the point arm. \textbf{Second, the fetch
set is highly cluster-local.} The embeddings' $\sim$1{,}015 fetched
points occupy an average of 27 of 1024 clusters, while uniform placement
would touch about 644. SIFT's 520 points occupy 50 clusters, against
about 408 under uniform placement. The reason
is that the nearest neighbors of a query are near each other. This
locality is what makes the cluster-major arm viable at all. Whether it
pays depends on the fetch-set size: the block arm beats the point arm only
on DEEP10M. \textbf{Third, point-versus-block is a device property.} The
point arm issues one read at a time. Its scattered small fetches beat the
block arm's fewer but heavier reads on three of the four datasets; only
DEEP10M's fetch set flips the order. The mean fetch counts in
Table~\ref{tab:ooc} are dominated by a minority of far-neighbor queries.
In a refit of each index, the median query fetches 148 vectors on SIFT1M,
2{,}334 on GIST1M, 110 on DBpedia-OpenAI, and 5{,}124 on DEEP10M, against
means of 520, 12{,}360, 1{,}015, and 44{,}597. This is why the median times sit well below the
mean read count times the per-read latency. Parallel small reads would extend the
point arm's advantage, since NVMe queue depths reward exactly that
pattern. Seek-bound media would flip every row toward block. The layout
writes both files, so the diagnostic should pick per device.

One design lesson surfaced only here: \emph{cascade depth matters far more
out of core than in RAM}. On SIFT1M the eigen-curve rule stops at
$(8,16,40)$, because the 85\% target is reached at 40 dimensions. Those
levels fetch 3{,}132 points. The deeper $(8,16,32,64)$ ladder fetches only
526 and wins the cold-read benchmark (78 vs.\ 106\,ms for the point arm). In RAM the same
change saves only 5\% of the terms (10.3 vs.\ 10.9\,M), because a
finalist costs $d$ terms there but a random read here. The
level-selection objective should follow the medium.

ADSampling~\cite{adsampling} is the natural adversary. It also prunes
with incremental prefix distances after a rotation. However, its rotation
is random, so partial distances become unbiased samples, and its stopping
rule is a sequential hypothesis test with a tunable error rate
$\epsilon_0$. This buys harder pruning per dimension, at the price of the
exactness guarantee. For a fair comparison, we ported the reference
implementation into the same harness and the same currency: dimensions
touched equal terms. In RAM, our cascade does less work on three of the
four datasets, by $1.2$--$3.2\times$ (Table~\ref{tab:ads}); ADS wins on
the 1536-D embeddings by $1.7\times$. At $k{=}10$ the split is the same:
ADS wins only on DBpedia-OpenAI ($1.8\times$). On recall, we must correct
our own first measurement. An earlier float32 ground-truth check
miscounted near-duplicate points as recall failures, due to catastrophic
cancellation. Under float64 verification, ADS at the default $\epsilon_0$
achieved perfect recall on every $k{=}1$ query we ran. It missed one true
neighbor in 800 query-tasks (GIST, $k{=}10$, recall 0.9990). The
distinction that matters is therefore \emph{guarantee versus empirical},
not observed failure. The distinction is structural, because no
$\epsilon_0$ turns the test into a bound.

The two methods also differ out of core. ADS pays its $\Delta d = 32$
floor for \emph{every} point in the dataset. SIFT's 33.0M terms and
DEEP10M's 325.8M terms sit just above $32n$. ADS therefore touches every
vector, and without a resident sketch it would read full-scan bytes
regardless of its parameters. With the two-tier layout we give our own
cascade, however, its first test could run on a resident 32-dimensional
sketch (128\,B/vector, less than our 256\,B/vector on SIFT1M), and only
the candidates that pass would be fetched. We have not measured that
configuration. PDX~\cite{pdx} restructures the layout dimension-major,
but it still visits every vector. Our cascade fetches only 0.05--1.2\% of
the vectors (Table~\ref{tab:ooc}). Finally, the two methods compose rather
than compete: a statistical test could accelerate the final full-distance
checks over our survivor sets, at the usual price.

\section{Limitations}

High \emph{intrinsic} dimensionality defeats every cheap bound. Distance
concentration flattens the eigenvalue spectrum and the spread of $d_p$ at
the same time. The funnel exists precisely to detect this case and say so
(Table~\ref{tab:regimes}, last row). DEEP10M sits near this boundary in
RAM. At native-code constants, the in-RAM wall-clock race belongs to
fused exhaustive scans (FAISS) at the 128-D scale. ADSampling's
statistical pruning also beats our deterministic bounds in RAM on the
highest-dimensional embeddings (\S\ref{sec:ads}). The in-RAM $k$-NN
gain shrinks with $k$, to $1.2\times$ on DEEP10M at $k=100$
(\S\ref{sec:knn-eval}), and large-$k$ queries out of core are
unmeasured.
Our out-of-core scan baseline does not overlap I/O with compute, so
speedups against it overstate the gain over a pipelined scan
(\S\ref{sec:ooc}). The out-of-core measurements use
\texttt{O\_DIRECT} on datasets that fit in RAM. This is valid
device-price accounting, but a larger-than-RAM run, with parallel rather
than serial fetch I/O, remains future work. Updates are also future work:
the cluster-major layout assumes a static index that is rebuilt
periodically, which is the same assumption disk-ANN builds make. Finally,
ball-bound and layout quality depend on the $k$-means sample, as any
learned structure does. Correctness never does.

\section{Conclusion}

Exact lower bounds are shapes, and shapes compose. The right composition
is a property of the data, and it can be measured on a sample before any
index is built. On that principle, this paper delivers an exact $k$-NN
index that configures itself end to end. The cascade widths come from the
eigenvalue curve; without this step, 1536-dimensional embeddings
masquerade as unprunable. The levels come from a measured trial, and its
sample verdicts are confirmed at $10^6$--$10^7$ scale on four real
datasets. The breaking points are DEEP10M's already-compressed features,
where the in-RAM gain is only $1.2$--$1.3\times$, and i.i.d.\ data
everywhere. The out-of-core argument has become a measurement: exact
cold-read queries run $2.5$--$19\times$ faster than our disk scan
($1.4$--$11\times$ against the device's sequential limit) and read
$18$--$730\times$ fewer bytes.
The recurring lesson of this work is simple. Every constant we had
silently tuned to one dataset---the cascade widths, the verdict
thresholds, even our own ground-truth checker---failed on the next
dataset. Every fix was the same: derive the constant from a measurement.
Measure the data to choose the index; count the work to predict the time.
